# Atlas Research Labs — Manifesto

## Our Belief

Markets reward superior decision-making, not merely superior information.

Information is increasingly commoditized.

Financial statements, market prices, news, technical indicators and macroeconomic data are available to an increasingly large number of participants.

The competitive advantage therefore lies not simply in possessing information, but in transforming information into better decisions.

---

## Our Mission

Atlas Research Labs exists to systematically discover, test and validate how information can be transformed into better investment decisions in the Indian equity market.

---

## Our Principles

### 1. Data before models

Reliable data is more valuable than sophisticated algorithms operating on unreliable data.

### 2. Evidence before intuition

A plausible story is not evidence.

### 3. Research before optimization

We seek to understand why something works before optimizing how well it works.

### 4. Simplicity before complexity

A complex model must demonstrate incremental value over a simpler alternative.

### 5. Out-of-sample before celebration

Historical performance is not sufficient evidence.

### 6. Risk before return

A strategy is judged by the return it generates relative to the risk required to obtain it.

### 7. Probability before certainty

Markets are uncertain. Our models should express uncertainty rather than pretend it does not exist.

### 8. Reproducibility before publication

Every important result should be capable of being reproduced.

### 9. Failure is information

A rejected hypothesis is a useful research result if it prevents us from repeating the same mistake.

### 10. Continuous learning

No model is assumed to remain optimal forever.

---

## The Objective

The objective is not to predict every movement in the market.

The objective is to determine whether a systematic, explainable and evidence-based process can consistently make better investment decisions than appropriate benchmarks.

---

## The Standard

We will not ask:

> "Can we make the backtest look good?"

We will ask:

> "Would we trust this result if we had to put real capital behind it?"

That distinction defines Atlas.
